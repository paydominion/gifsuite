All four can show a short looping clip. They differ in file size, quality and where they work.

## The short answer

| You want to… | Use |
|---|---|
| Post on X, WhatsApp or Instagram | **MP4** |
| Drop it into an email, a doc or an old forum | **GIF** |
| Put it on your own website | **WebM** or **MP4** |
| Make a sticker with a see-through background | **WebP** |

## How they compare

**GIF** dates from 1987. It's limited to 256 colors per frame and compresses poorly, but it plays almost anywhere an image can be shown, with no play button.

**MP4** (H.264) is a real video format. It keeps full color and is dramatically smaller. It needs a video player, which every phone, browser and social app has.

**WebM** (VP9) is an open video format built for the web. It's about as small as MP4 and can keep a transparent background.

**WebP** is an image format that can animate. It has full color and smooth transparency, and sits between GIF and video in size. All current browsers support it; some older apps don't.

## How big is the difference?

In one of our tests, the same 4-second clip at the same size came out as:

- GIF: 654 KB
- WebP: 155 KB
- WebM: 39 KB
- MP4: 26 KB

The GIF was 25 times the size of the MP4. That gap is why most social apps quietly turn uploaded GIFs into video.
