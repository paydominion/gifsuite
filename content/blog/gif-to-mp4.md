We took a 1.6-second cat GIF from Tenor, 498 by 476 pixels and 2.2 MB, and converted it to MP4 at the same size and frame rate. The MP4 was 140 KB, about 15 times smaller, and looked the same.

## Why is an MP4 so much smaller than a GIF?

A GIF stores every frame as a complete picture, compressed one at a time, with at most 256 colors. A video format like MP4 stores mostly what changes from one frame to the next, and its compression was designed for moving pictures. For an animation, that difference is huge.

## How to convert a GIF to MP4

1. Open [Upload & convert](/tools/convert).
2. Drop in your GIF.
3. Choose **MP4** and a quality.
4. Download the result.

Your file is processed and sent straight back; it isn't stored. If the GIF is on a website instead of your computer, paste its link on the [GifSuite home page](/) and download it as MP4 from there.

## Which quality should you pick?

These are the sizes we got from that 2.2 MB GIF:

| Quality | What it does | Size |
|---|---|---|
| High | Keeps the full size and frame rate | 140 KB |
| Medium | Up to 480 pixels wide, 15 frames a second | 50 KB |
| Low | Up to 320 pixels wide, 10 frames a second | 14 KB |

High is the right choice for posting. Medium and Low suit quick sends on slow connections.

## When should you keep the GIF?

An MP4 is a video, so it needs a video player. Keep the GIF for email, documents, forums and anywhere that only shows images. For chats and social media, MP4 is usually better: X and WhatsApp play short clips like GIFs. See [GIF size limits by platform](/blog/gif-size-limits-by-platform) for where each one fits.
